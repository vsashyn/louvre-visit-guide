# The Frieze of Archers from the Palace of Darius at Susa

Achaemenid Persian, c. 510 BC. Richelieu wing, Level 0, in the Iranian galleries.

## In one line

The only place in the museum where the ancient world is still in full colour.

## What you are looking at

A line of guardsmen in profile, life-size, walking to the right. Each holds a spear vertically in both hands, with a bow and a quiver slung on his back. They wear long patterned robes in yellow, white, green and brown, and twisted headbands. Their beards and hair are rendered in tight regular curls.

The frieze is made of glazed brick. Each brick was moulded with part of a figure in low relief, glazed, fired and then assembled like a jigsaw. To stop the colours running into each other in the kiln, the makers built thin raised ridges, cloisons, between the glaze fields.

## The story

Darius I built a palace at Susa and left an inscription describing where each material and each craftsman came from: cedar from Lebanon, gold from Sardis and Bactria, ivory from Ethiopia, and Babylonian workers for the brick. It was a deliberate demonstration that the empire's whole reach had gone into the building.

The frieze was excavated by the French mission led by Marcel and Jane Dieulafoy in 1884–1886. Jane Dieulafoy was an archaeologist, novelist and photographer who worked in men's clothing with a permit from the French government, and her account of the dig is worth reading.

### Are they the Immortals?

Probably not exactly. Herodotus describes an elite corps of 10,000 called the Immortals, kept permanently at full strength by replacing every casualty at once. It is tempting to identify these guards with them, and museums often do. The evidence is circumstantial. What is certain is that they are royal guards, shown as an endless repeating procession.

## Look for

- The cloisons. Get close and you can see the raised lines that separate the colours.
- The robe patterns, different on different figures.
- The individual bricks and the joins.
- The feet. Each guard steps with the same foot forward, which makes the line read as a single moving body.

## Interesting facts

- Glazed brick was a Babylonian technology. The Ishtar Gate in Berlin uses the same method a century earlier.
- The Louvre's Achaemenid holdings from Susa are among the largest outside Iran.
- The nearby reconstruction of a column capital from the apadana, the great audience hall, shows two bull foreparts back to back. It gives you the scale of the building these guards were painted on.

## Talking point

Everything else from the ancient world in this museum has lost its colour. This has not. Ask what the Parthenon frieze would look like if it had.
