# The Virgin and Child of Jeanne d'Évreux

Parisian, before 1339. Richelieu wing, Level 1, medieval decorative arts.

## In one line

Sixty-eight centimetres of silver gilt holding the Virgin's milk, her hair and scraps of her clothing inside a crystal lily.

## What you are looking at

The Virgin stands with her weight on one hip in the deep S-curve of Parisian Gothic, the Christ child on her left arm. Her body is silver, fire-gilded to gold. In her right hand she holds a fleur-de-lis of gilded silver and rock crystal.

She stands on a rectangular base decorated with fourteen scenes from the Passion in translucent enamel over engraved silver, a technique called basse-taille, where the silver is carved in low relief and then covered in coloured glass so that the image glows from beneath.

### The relics

The crystal lily is a reliquary. It contained relics associated with the Virgin: fragments of her clothing, hair, and milk.

Reliquaries in the form of a standing Virgin were a Parisian speciality. The object is simultaneously a devotional image, a precious work of goldsmith's art and a container for holy matter.

## The story

Jeanne d'Évreux was the third wife of Charles IV of France, the last Capetian king. When he died in 1328 without a male heir, the succession crisis that followed became one of the causes of the Hundred Years' War.

In 1339 Jeanne gave the abbey of Saint-Denis her crown, a reliquary of relics from the Sainte-Chapelle, and two reliquary statuettes, of which this is one. The donation is recorded in an inscription on the base.

Saint-Denis was the royal necropolis, and the gift is both piety and dynastic assertion.

## Look for

- The enamels on the base. Fourteen small Passion scenes, the earliest dated French translucent enamels. Get as close as the case allows.
- The S-curve of the body, called the Gothic sway. The Virgin's weight is on one foot and the whole figure bends.
- The child, reaching for his mother's face.
- The crystal lily, and the space inside it.
- The pearls and stones on the base.

## Interesting facts

- The object is among the most important surviving pieces of fourteenth-century Parisian goldsmiths' work, from a period when Paris led Europe in the craft.
- Almost all comparable royal plate was melted down in wars and revolutions. Survival at this level is rare.
- Saint-Denis was looted in the Revolution and the royal tombs were emptied. This piece came out.

## Talking point

Everything else Jeanne d'Évreux owned was melted for the metal. Ask why this one thing survived.
