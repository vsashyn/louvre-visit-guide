# The Coronation of the Virgin

Fra Angelico, c. 1434–1435. Denon wing, Level 1, in the early Italian rooms near the Salon Carré.

## In one line

A Dominican friar's vision of heaven, with a floor made of light and a crowd of saints you can identify one by one.

## What you are looking at

Christ crowns the Virgin at the top of a flight of steps. Around and below them, ranks of saints, martyrs and angels kneel on a golden ground, arranged in a wide arc that opens toward you like an amphitheatre.

Fra Angelico was a friar as well as a painter, and the picture is built as an object of devotion rather than a display of skill. The colours are jewel-clear because he used expensive pigments over a smooth gesso ground and finished with gold leaf and punched decoration.

## The story

Painted for the convent of San Domenico at Fiesole, outside Florence, where Fra Angelico was a friar. John Pope-Hennessy argued instead for a later date around Angelico's Roman period of 1450.

It came to France with the Napoleonic seizures. Like the Veronese, it was never returned, and the excuse given was its size.

## Look for

- The rainbow of haloes, each one a disc of gold incised with a different pattern.
- The individual saints. Dominic in black and white, Catherine with her wheel, Lawrence with his gridiron, Peter Martyr with the blade in his skull.
- The steps. Fra Angelico builds real perspective into a gold-ground painting, which is a hybrid of two incompatible systems and works anyway.

## Interesting facts

- Fra Angelico painted a second Coronation of the Virgin, around 1432, now in the Uffizi.
- He was beatified by John Paul II in 1982 and is the patron of artists.
- The gold is real gold leaf laid on red bole, which is why it has that warm tone where it has worn.

## Talking point

Count the different halo patterns. No two ranks are punched the same way.
