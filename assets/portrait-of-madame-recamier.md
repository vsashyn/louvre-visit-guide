# Portrait of Madame Récamier

Jacques-Louis David, 1800. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 702 (Salle Daru).

## In one line

Unfinished because the sitter kept turning up late and then hired David's own student instead.

## What you are looking at

Juliette Récamier, 22, barefoot in a white Empire dress on a backless sofa in an almost empty room. One antique-style lamp on the right. Bare floor, bare wall, nothing else.

Her head is fully worked up, hair and complexion finished to a high polish. Everything else, the dress, the couch, the floor, is loose paint over visible ground. You are looking at a David in mid-construction.

## The story

Juliette Récamier ran one of the most important salons in Paris, a meeting point for writers and for opponents of Napoleon, including Chateaubriand and Madame de Staël. Her furniture is now in the Louvre's decorative arts collection.

She was persistently late for sittings. David lost patience. She commissioned his pupil François Gérard instead, who finished a portrait of her in 1805. David never went back to this one and reportedly told her that ladies have their caprices and so do painters.

The state acquired it in 1826, the year after David died.

## Look for

- The transition where finish stops. It happens at the neckline.
- The bare feet, a deliberate classicising touch that was slightly scandalous.
- The horizontal format, unusual for a portrait at the time.
- The emptiness. David strips out every prop a normal society portrait would include.

## Interesting facts

- The backless sofa in this painting gave its name to the récamier, still the standard term for that piece of furniture.
- René Magritte replaced her with a coffin in his 1950 Perspective: Madame Récamier by David.
- Ingres borrowed the pose for La Grande Odalisque, hanging nearby.

## Talking point

Unfinished, it is more modern than anything else in the room. Ask whether finishing it would have improved it.
