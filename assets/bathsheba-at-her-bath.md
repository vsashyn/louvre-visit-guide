# Bathsheba at Her Bath

Rembrandt van Rijn, 1654. Richelieu wing, Level 2, in the Rembrandt rooms.

## In one line

A woman holding a letter she cannot refuse, painted by a man who removed the king, the palace and the drama and left only the decision.

## What you are looking at

Bathsheba sits naked after bathing, holding a letter loosely in her right hand. An old servant kneels at her feet drying them. Her body is turned toward you and her face is turned down and away, unfocused, thinking.

The painting is a perfect square, 142 by 142 cm.

## The story

2 Samuel 11. King David sees Bathsheba bathing from the roof of his palace, sends for her, and she becomes pregnant. To hide it, David has her husband Uriah placed at the front of a battle and abandoned there to be killed.

Almost every earlier painter chose the voyeuristic moment, with David visible on his roof. Rembrandt removes David entirely. There is no palace, no king, no witnesses except the servant, who is looking at feet.

That leaves the viewer in David's position. You are the one looking.

### The medical debate

Physicians have written about the left breast for over a century. Proposals have included breast cancer, a tubercular abscess, and mastitis. Professor Paolo Zamboni argued for thrombophlebitis of a superficial breast vein, the condition described by Mondor in 1939, based on comparison with ultrasound images.

Hendrickje Stoffels, Rembrandt's partner, is traditionally identified as the model. She would have been about 28 in 1654. She died in 1663, probably of plague. Whether Rembrandt painted a real pathology or simply painted a real body is unresolved.

## Look for

- The letter. It is the summons. Her fingers are barely holding it.
- The face. X-rays show Rembrandt lowered her head from a more upright, watchful position, and added the letter later. He changed a woman who was alert into a woman who has already understood.
- The left breast. There is a visible swelling and dimpling of the skin.
- The light, which falls only on her body and the linen and leaves everything else in brown.

## Interesting facts

- Kenneth Clark called it Rembrandt's greatest painting of the nude.
- Rembrandt began the composition around 1647 and finished it in 1654.
- The canvas may have been trimmed by 10 to 20 cm.
- Dr Louis La Caze left it to the Louvre in his 1869 bequest, one of the largest private gifts the museum has ever received.
- Manet and Degas both studied it.

## Talking point

Rembrandt gives her a decision and does not tell you what she decides. Stand there long enough and you will start guessing.
