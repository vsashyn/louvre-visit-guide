# Gabrielle d'Estrées and One of Her Sisters

School of Fontainebleau, c. 1594. Richelieu wing, Level 2, French sixteenth-century painting.

## In one line

Two naked women in a bath, one pinching the other's nipple, staring straight out at you, and it is a pregnancy announcement.

## What you are looking at

Two women sit side by side in a bath behind a red curtain. Both are nude to the waist, both wear pearls and elaborate jewellery, both look directly out of the picture with completely neutral expressions. The woman on the right holds the left one's right nipple between thumb and forefinger. The woman on the left holds up a ring between her fingers.

In the background, a fire burns and a woman sits sewing.

## The story

Gabrielle d'Estrées was the mistress of Henri IV. The woman beside her is traditionally identified as her sister Julienne-Hippolyte-Joséphine, Duchess of Villars.

The nipple pinch is a coded announcement of pregnancy. Gabrielle bore Henri three children, including César de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme, born 1594.

The ring is thought to be Henri's coronation ring, given to her as a token. Henri intended to marry her once his marriage to Marguerite de Valois was annulled, which would have made a royal mistress queen of France.

The seamstress in the background is probably making a layette for the child.

Gabrielle died in 1599, at 26, after a convulsive fit while pregnant with their fourth child, weeks before the planned wedding. Henri married Marie de' Medici instead, and her cycle by Rubens hangs a few rooms away in this same wing.

## Look for

- The expressions. Blank. Neither woman gives you anything.
- The ring.
- The seamstress, in ordinary dress, going about her work behind a scene that is not ordinary at all.
- The red curtain, which is a stage curtain. You are being shown something deliberately.

## Interesting facts

- A clothed version was produced shortly afterwards.
- During the nineteenth century the painting hung in the Paris Prefecture of Police behind a green curtain, classified as indecent.
- Modern viewers routinely read it as an image of female homoeroticism, which is not what a 1594 audience would have read, but the painting is genuinely strange enough to support it.
- The German Green Party used the composition on a poster supporting same-sex marriage in 2002.
- The artist is unknown. School of Fontainebleau covers the Italian-influenced court style at Francis I's palace and its later French practitioners.

## Talking point

It is a birth announcement, a dynastic claim and a piece of erotica in one panel. Rank them by which the painter cared about most.
