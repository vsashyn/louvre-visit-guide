# The Ray

Jean-Siméon Chardin, 1728. Sully wing, Level 2, French eighteenth-century painting.

## In one line

A gutted skate hanging on a hook, with a face, and it got a 29-year-old into the Academy on the same day he applied.

## What you are looking at

A kitchen table. On the left, a cat picks its way over a pile of oysters, back arched, about to be caught. In the centre, a large skate hangs split open on a hook, its pale wet interior facing you, the gill slits arranged into what is unmistakably a grinning human face. On the right, a copper pot, a knife, a jug, a cloth.

The pyramid composition puts the skate at the apex, and its ghastly smile is the most memorable thing in the French eighteenth-century rooms.

## The story

Chardin first showed it outdoors at the Exposition de la Jeunesse on 3 June 1728, a free annual open-air show at the Place Dauphine and the Pont Neuf on Corpus Christi. On 25 September 1728 he presented it to the Académie Royale along with The Buffet. The Academy accepted both as his reception pieces and admitted him the same day, which was unusually fast.

Still life sat near the bottom of the Academy's hierarchy of genres. Chardin spent his career there and made it look like the only place worth being.

## Look for

- The face. Once you see it you will never see a skate again without it.
- The cat. It is in mid-step and its balance is perfect.
- The oysters, each one a different colour.
- The paint itself. Chardin built surfaces by dabbing and stippling, and in places he used his fingers rather than a brush.

## Interesting facts

- Diderot, who admired Chardin enormously, called this painting disgusting.
- Marcel Proust wrote about it, comparing the split skate to the nave of a polychrome cathedral.
- Matisse copied it.
- The painting stayed with the Academy until the Revolution, then passed to the Muséum Central des Arts, which became the Louvre.

## Talking point

Chardin was a still-life painter in a century that considered still life barely art, and this is the picture he chose to introduce himself with.
