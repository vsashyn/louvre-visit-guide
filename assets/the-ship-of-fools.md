# The Ship of Fools

Hieronymus Bosch, c. 1490–1500. Richelieu wing, Level 2, in the early Netherlandish rooms.

## In one line

The only Bosch in the Louvre, and it is two-thirds of a plank sawn off a dismembered altarpiece.

## What you are looking at

A small boat with a tree for a mast, drifting. On board: a nun and a monk singing over a lute, a man climbing the mast toward a roast fowl tied to it, another vomiting over the side, a swimmer begging for drink, a jester sitting apart in the rigging with his own cup.

A large flat cake or pancake hangs from a string. Two people are trying to bite it with their hands tied behind their backs, which was a real fairground game.

Nobody is steering. There are no oars in the water.

## The story

The ship of fools is an old trope, going back through Plato's Republic Book VI, but it exploded in popularity after Sebastian Brant published Das Narrenschiff in Basel in 1494. Dendrochronology dates the panel's wood to a tree felled in 1491, which allows Bosch to be responding directly to Brant's book.

The panel is a fragment. The original was a wing of a triptych on the seven deadly sins. The bottom third of this same plank is in the Yale University Art Gallery, catalogued as Allegory of Gluttony. The other wing is Death and the Miser in the National Gallery of Art in Washington. The exterior panel, The Wayfarer, is in Rotterdam. Wood analysis shows all the surviving panels came from the same tree.

The central panel, if there was one, is unknown.

## Look for

- The owl in the tree-mast. Bosch's owls are never good news.
- The jester, in the rigging at the left, apart from everyone and drinking alone. In Brant, the fool who knows he is a fool is the only one with any hope.
- The banner with a crescent, which some read as an anti-Turkish jab and others as simply a pennant.
- The knife stuck in the tree.
- The two clerics, a monk and a nun, running the entertainment.

## Interesting facts

- Camille Benoit gave it to the Louvre in 1918. It was restored in 2015.
- A later drawn copy of the composition is also in the Louvre.
- Bosch painted perhaps 25 surviving works. Most are in Madrid, Lisbon and Venice.
- The cake-biting game shown here was played at fairs into the twentieth century.

## Talking point

Bosch's boat has no rudder and no destination. Ask what the nun and monk are doing in it.
