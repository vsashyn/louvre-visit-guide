# The Death of the Virgin

Caravaggio, c. 1601–1606. Denon wing, Level 1, Grande Galerie, in the Italian seventeenth-century rooms.

## In one line

Rejected by the priests who paid for it because the Virgin looked too much like a dead woman.

## What you are looking at

Mary lies on a plank bed in a bare room, her feet bare and swollen, her arm hanging off the edge, her belly distended. A huge red curtain fills the upper third of the canvas and does nothing to comfort anyone. The apostles crowd around, most of them with their faces hidden or turned away. In the foreground a young woman, Mary Magdalene, sits doubled over with her head in her hands.

There is no ascension, no angels, no light from heaven. Just a body and a room full of grief.

## The story

Painted for the Carmelite church of Santa Maria della Scala in Trastevere, Rome. The fathers refused it. Giovanni Baglione recorded the complaint: the Virgin was shown "swollen-up and with bare legs". Giulio Mancini added the rumour that Caravaggio had used a prostitute, possibly his own mistress, as the model. Another version of the story says the model was a woman pulled from the Tiber.

Rubens, then in Rome, thought it one of Caravaggio's best paintings and pushed Vincenzo Gonzaga, Duke of Mantua, to buy it in 1607. Before it left Rome it was put on public show for a week and the city's painters queued to see it.

From Mantua it went to Charles I of England in 1627, was auctioned after his execution in 1649, bought by Eberhard Jabach, and sold to Louis XIV in 1671.

## Look for

- The red curtain. It is the only theatrical element, and Caravaggio uses it as a lid pressing down on the scene.
- The copper basin in the foreground. Someone has been washing the body.
- Mary's bare feet, the specific detail that got it rejected.
- The near-absence of faces among the apostles. Grief shown by backs and hands.

## Interesting facts

- Caravaggio killed Ranuccio Tomassoni in a brawl in May 1606 and fled Rome with a price on his head. He never saw the public reaction to this painting.
- Carlo Saraceni painted a replacement altarpiece for the church, and it is still there.
- Rubens is the reason this painting survived in a major collection rather than being cut up or lost.

## Talking point

Find the halo. It is a thin, almost apologetic line over her head, the only supernatural mark in the entire canvas.
