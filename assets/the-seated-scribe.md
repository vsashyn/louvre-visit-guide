# The Seated Scribe

Egyptian, Old Kingdom, c. 2620–2350 BC. Sully wing, Level 1, Room 635.

## In one line

Four and a half thousand years old, and he is looking at you, waiting for you to start dictating.

## What you are looking at

A man sits cross-legged with a partly unrolled papyrus across his lap. His right hand is positioned to hold a reed pen, now lost. His torso is upright and alert. His face is turned slightly up and out, attentive, mouth about to move.

He is not idealised. The chest is a little slack, the belly has a fold, the shoulders are set with the specific posture of a man who sits to work.

Then there are the eyes, and the eyes are why people stop.

### How the eyes are made

Each eye is built from several materials. The white is red-veined magnesite. The cornea is rock crystal, polished and slightly convex. Behind the crystal, a small pad of organic material creates the iris, which reads as blue-grey. A polished copper-arsenic alloy frame forms the eyelids and the line of the lashes.

The effect is that the pupils catch light like living eyes, and they appear to follow you across the room.

## The story

Auguste Mariette dug him up at Saqqara on 19 November 1850, north of the line of sphinxes leading to the Serapeum. Mariette did not record the exact findspot, which is a real loss.

Nobody knows who he is. The base is semicircular, which suggests it originally fitted into a larger monument that would have carried his name.

## Look for

- The eyes, from several distances and angles.
- The hand positions. One holds the pen, the other steadies the papyrus.
- The paint. Red-brown on the skin, black on the hair, white on the kilt. It is original.
- The wooden nipples, inlaid separately.
- The difference between the face and the body. The face is intensely individual, the body more generalised.

## Interesting facts

- The cross-legged scribe pose may have been restricted to members of the royal family in the Old Kingdom, which would make this man very senior.
- Literacy in Old Kingdom Egypt was confined to a tiny elite. A scribe was an administrator, and administrators ran the state.
- Similar figures exist, notably in Cairo, but none with eyes in this condition.

## Talking point

Stand to one side, then walk slowly across in front of him. Decide at what point the eyes stop following you.
