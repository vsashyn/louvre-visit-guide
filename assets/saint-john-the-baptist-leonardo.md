# Saint John the Baptist

Leonardo da Vinci, c. 1513–1516. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 710 (Grande Galerie).

## In one line

Probably the last painting Leonardo made, and the strangest.

## What you are looking at

A figure emerges out of near-total blackness, lit as if by a single source close to the left. He wears animal skins, holds a thin reed cross over his shoulder, and points straight up with his right index finger. He is smiling at you.

By convention John the Baptist is painted gaunt, weathered, ascetic. Leonardo painted him soft, young, curly-haired and deliberately ambiguous in gender.

## The story

Leonardo kept it with him to France. It was in Francis I's collection at Fontainebleau by 1542. In 1625 Louis XIII traded it to Charles I of England for a Titian and a Holbein. When Charles was executed and his collection sold off in 1649, the Cologne banker Eberhard Jabach bought it, and Jabach sold it back into the French royal collection under Louis XIV in 1661. It has been in Paris ever since.

## Look for

- The upward-pointing finger. In Leonardo's late work this gesture appears again and again, always meaning something beyond the frame.
- How little there is. No landscape, no props, no setting. Just a body coming out of darkness.
- The reed cross, so thin it almost disappears against the dark.

## Interesting facts

- Salaì, Leonardo's long-term pupil and companion, is widely thought to have modelled for this and for the related Bacchus figures.
- Kenneth Clark called the painting "the eternal question mark, the enigma of creation".
- Leonardo's youthful John influenced Raphael's workshop within a year or two of his death and changed how the saint was painted afterwards.
- It was cleaned and restored in 2016, which lifted a great deal of yellowed varnish.
- It went on loan to Louvre Abu Dhabi for two years from November 2022 for that museum's fifth anniversary, so check it is back before you plan around it.

## Talking point

Cover the cross with your hand. Without it, almost nothing in the picture says "saint".
