# The Wedding Feast at Cana

Paolo Veronese, 1562–1563. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 711, directly opposite the Mona Lisa.

## In one line

The largest painting in the Louvre, ignored by almost everyone in the room because it is behind them.

## What you are looking at

Christ's first miracle, turning water into wine at a wedding in Cana, staged as a Venetian banquet. Around 130 figures crowd a marble terrace under a Palladian colonnade. Christ sits at the centre of the table, small and still, while the party roars around him.

Veronese did not paint first-century Galilee. He painted the Venice of 1563, with its clothes, its glassware, its dwarfs and its dogs.

## The story

The Benedictine monks of San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice commissioned it for their refectory, so that the monks would eat their silent meals facing a feast. The contract paid 324 ducats plus board, wine and lodging for Veronese and his brother Benedetto, and specified the best pigments, including expensive lapis lazuli ultramarine.

On 11 September 1797 Napoleon's soldiers took it. To move it they cut the canvas in half horizontally and rolled it. After Waterloo, when the allies forced France to return looted art, the Louvre's director Vivant Denon claimed the canvas was too fragile to travel. Antonio Canova, sent by the Pope to recover Italian works, accepted the excuse. Venice got a Charles Le Brun in exchange and never got Veronese back.

## Look for

- The four musicians in the foreground. Tradition identifies them as Veronese in white playing viola da braccio, Titian in red on the violone, Tintoretto on a second viola, and Jacopo Bassano on the cornetto. Painters cast as the band at Christ's miracle.
- The hourglass on the table between the musicians, right below Christ. Time is running.
- Above Christ's head, a butcher carving a lamb on the balustrade, a direct sign of the Lamb of God.
- The servant at far right pouring the new wine, dark red, out of a jug. That is the miracle itself, happening off to the side while nobody watches.
- The Ottoman and European rulers in the crowd, painted as guests: Suleiman the Magnificent, Charles V, Francis I, Mary I of England.

## Interesting facts

- At 67.3 square metres it is the biggest painting the Louvre owns.
- It hung 2.5 metres above the refectory floor, so the perspective is built to be seen from below. In the Salle des États you are looking at it more or less straight on, which is not how Veronese intended.
- In June 1992 a support frame gave way while the 1.5 tonne canvas was being raised. It tore in five places. All the damage fell on architecture, not faces.
- The 1989–1992 restoration caused a public fight. Removing later red overpaint from the steward's tabard revealed the original green, and a heritage association protested.
- In 2007, 210 years after the looting, the Giorgio Cini Foundation and the Louvre installed a full-size digital facsimile back in the San Giorgio refectory. Venice now has a perfect copy of its own painting.

## Practical

This is the best trade in the museum. Everyone in Room 711 faces one way. Turn around, sit on the bench, and you get a 67 square metre masterpiece almost to yourself.

## Talking point

Find Christ. It takes most people twenty seconds, which is exactly the point Veronese was making about how a miracle lands in a noisy room.
