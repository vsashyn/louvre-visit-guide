# An Old Man and his Grandson

Domenico Ghirlandaio, c. 1490. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 710 (Grande Galerie).

## In one line

The most tender picture in the Italian galleries, and it puts a disfiguring skin disease at dead centre.

## What you are looking at

An old man in red holds a small boy who leans into his chest and looks up at him. The old man's nose is swollen and lumpy with rhinophyma, an advanced form of rosacea. Ghirlandaio paints it precisely, without softening and without cruelty. Through a window on the right, a winding road climbs to a hill.

The boy's hand rests on the old man's chest. Neither of them is performing for you.

## The story

The identities are unknown, and even the family relationship is an assumption. A silverpoint drawing in the Nationalmuseum in Stockholm shows the same head with the eyes closed, which has led to the suggestion that Ghirlandaio drew the man after death and built the painting around it.

The Louvre acquired it in 1880, after the Kaiser Friedrich Museum in Berlin turned it down over its condition.

## Look for

- The boy's hand and the old man's hand. Neither is gripping.
- The window landscape, a Netherlandish device that Italian painters had adopted by this date.
- The damage. The face was overcleaned and scratched in the nineteenth century, and the painting was fully restored in 1996.

## Interesting facts

- Bernard Berenson wrote that "there is no more human picture in the entire range of Quattrocento painting, whether in or out of Italy."
- Ghirlandaio ran the busiest workshop in Florence. A teenage Michelangelo was apprenticed to him.
- Rhinophyma was, and is, often wrongly blamed on drinking. Ghirlandaio makes no moral point about it at all.

## Talking point

This is the painting most people remember from the Grande Galerie a week later, and it is a few steps from three Leonardos.
