# La Belle Jardinière

Raphael, 1507–1508. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 710 (Grande Galerie).

## In one line

Raphael at twenty-four, taking everything he had just learned from Leonardo and making it look effortless.

## What you are looking at

Mary sits in a meadow in a blue cloak and red dress. The Christ child stands against her knee and looks up at her face. John the Baptist kneels at the left with his reed cross. Behind them, a soft Umbrian landscape with a lake and a town.

The three figures form a pyramid, the device Raphael took directly from Leonardo, whose work he had studied closely in Florence. What Raphael adds is warmth. Leonardo's groups are enigmatic. This one is a mother with two toddlers in a field.

## The story

Painted in Raphael's Florentine period, probably commissioned around 1507 by the Sienese patrician Fabrizio Sergardi. Raphael left Florence for Rome before it was finished, and Ridolfo del Ghirlandaio completed it, most notably Mary's blue robe.

Francis I brought it to France, where it was copied repeatedly and became one of the best-known Raphaels in Europe. The nickname, "the beautiful gardener", is French and dates from later.

## Look for

- The book in Mary's right hand, held half-closed with a finger marking the place. She has stopped reading to look at her son. The book is traditionally read as the prophecies of the Passion.
- Christ's foot resting on Mary's. A small physical detail that does a lot of work.
- The gold hem inscription on Mary's neckline, where Raphael signed and dated the picture.
- The blue of the robe, painted by someone else.

## Interesting facts

- Raphael made this immediately after the Madonna of the Goldfinch and just before the Madonna of the Meadow. The three are variations on the same problem.
- The debt to Leonardo's Virgin and Child with Saint Anne, hanging in the same wing, is direct. See them within a few minutes of each other.
- Raphael was in his mid-twenties here and had about twelve years left to live.

## Talking point

Raphael was 24 or 25 when he painted this. Leonardo was 51 and still had not finished the Saint Anne down the gallery.
