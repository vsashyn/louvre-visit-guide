# The Dendera Zodiac

Egyptian, c. 50 BC. Sully wing, Level 1, Egyptian antiquities, mounted overhead.

## In one line

A ceiling sawn out of an Egyptian temple with gunpowder, sold to a king, and it caused a theological crisis about the age of the world.

## What you are looking at

A circular sky map carved in sandstone. Two rings of figures surround a centre pole. The outer ring holds 36 decans, the ten-day units of the Egyptian calendar. Inside, the twelve zodiac signs appear in a mix of Greco-Roman and Egyptian forms: a Greek Aries and Taurus next to an Egyptian jackal for Ursa Minor and a hippopotamus for Draco. Twelve figures with raised arms support the whole disc.

It is the only complete map of the ancient sky that survives.

## The story

It was the ceiling of a chapel dedicated to Osiris on the roof of the temple of Hathor at Dendera. Vivant Denon recorded it during Napoleon's Egyptian campaign and published it in 1802.

In 1820 the antiquities dealer Sébastien Louis Saulnier hired the engineer Claude Lelorrain to get it out. Lelorrain used saws, jacks, scissors and gunpowder, cut the relief free, and shipped it to France. Louis XVIII bought it for 150,000 francs in 1821 for the Royal Library. It passed to the Louvre in 1922.

A plaster cast now fills the hole in the temple roof at Dendera.

### The scandal it caused

When French scholars first published the zodiac, several dated it to 15,000 BC on astronomical grounds. That was flatly incompatible with the biblical chronology then widely accepted, which put Creation around 4000 BC. A public argument ran for two decades. It was settled when Champollion read the hieroglyphs and dated it to the Roman period, which is where it sits today, around 50 BC.

## Look for

- The two eclipses. Researchers have identified a solar eclipse of 7 March 51 BC and a lunar eclipse of 25 September 52 BC encoded in the relief.
- The jackal at the centre, which is Ursa Minor, marking the north celestial pole.
- The Greek zodiac signs mixed in with Egyptian constellations. Two sky systems on one ceiling.
- The saw marks at the edges.

## Interesting facts

- Zahi Hawass formally requested its return to Egypt in 2022, alongside the Rosetta Stone and the Nefertiti bust.
- The cutting of a temple ceiling with gunpowder was controversial even in 1821. Champollion himself objected to the methods used in Egypt at the time.
- Because the zodiac itself is a Greek import, this relief is evidence of Babylonian and Greek astronomy arriving in Egypt.

## Talking point

The argument this object started, science versus scripture on the age of the world, was running thirty years before Darwin.
