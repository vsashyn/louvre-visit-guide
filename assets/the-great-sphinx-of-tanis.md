# The Great Sphinx of Tanis

Egyptian, date disputed. Sully wing, Level -1, Room 338, the Crypt of the Sphinx.

## In one line

Four and a half metres of pink granite that four different pharaohs claimed as their own by carving their names on it.

## What you are looking at

A lion's body with a king's head, in pink granite from Aswan, carved from a single block. The claws are extended. The body is tensed. The nemes headcloth frames a face whose features have been recut.

The lion is the sun and the sky god. The human head is the pharaoh. The sphinx guards a threshold, which is exactly what it does here: it sits at the entrance to the Louvre's Egyptian department, in front of more than 6,000 objects covering 5,000 years.

## The story

Found in 1825 in the ruins of the temple of Amun-Ra at Tanis in the Nile Delta, the capital of Egypt under the 21st and 22nd dynasties. The Louvre bought it in 1826 as part of the second Henry Salt collection.

Nobody knows who commissioned it. The earliest inscription names Amenemhat II of the 12th Dynasty. Later pharaohs added their own cartouches: Merneptah of the 19th Dynasty, and Shoshenq I of the 22nd. Some scholars argue the original carving is older still, from the 4th Dynasty around the 26th century BC.

## Look for

- The cartouches. Three sets of royal names on one object, added over more than a thousand years. Each pharaoh was appropriating it.
- The recut face. The features have been reworked, which is part of why dating is so contested.
- The granite. Aswan pink granite is extremely hard. Egyptian sculptors worked it with dolerite pounders and copper tools with abrasive sand.

## Interesting facts

- It stood in the museum courtyard from 1828 to 1848, then in the Galerie Henri IV.
- In the mid-1930s the Louvre architect Albert Ferran built the crypt specifically for it, and it has been there since.
- Reusing and re-inscribing monuments was completely normal in Egypt. Ramesses II did it on an industrial scale.

## Talking point

Three kings put their names on it over a millennium. Ask whether that counts as vandalism or as continuity.
