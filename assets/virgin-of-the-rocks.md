# The Virgin of the Rocks

Leonardo da Vinci, c. 1483–1486. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 710 (Grande Galerie).

## In one line

The painting Leonardo probably sold out from under the clients who commissioned it, then replaced with a second version after a lawsuit that dragged on for twenty-five years.

## What you are looking at

Four figures in a grotto: the Virgin, the infant John the Baptist kneeling in prayer on the left, the Christ child blessing him on the right, and the angel Uriel steadying Christ. They form a pyramid, the shape Leonardo used to lock a group together.

Nothing in Renaissance painting looked like this rock formation. Leonardo, who filled notebooks with geology and botany, painted a real cave with accurate plants and accurate light falling from an opening above.

## The story

On 25 April 1483 the Confraternity of the Immaculate Conception in Milan contracted Leonardo and the brothers Ambrogio and Evangelista de Predis to paint panels for an altarpiece in San Francesco Grande.

Then came the money. Leonardo and Ambrogio asked for 1,200 extra lire around 1490. The confraternity offered 100. A valuation in 1506 found the work still incomplete. The altarpiece was finally installed on 18 August 1508.

Most scholars think Leonardo sold this first version privately, probably to a buyer who paid better, and painted the second version now in London to satisfy the contract. This one first shows up in French royal records at Fontainebleau in 1625.

## Look for

- The angel's pointing finger. In this Louvre version Uriel points at John the Baptist and looks out of the picture at you. In the London version the pointing hand is gone.
- No haloes and no cross. In London both were added. Here the four figures are identified only by gesture, which is part of why the confraternity may have been unhappy.
- The plants. Botanists have identified specific species, painted accurately enough to name.
- The yellowed varnish. The Louvre has deliberately not stripped it. Kenneth Clark complained in 1939 that "we can form no real conception of the colour", and that is still broadly true.

## Interesting facts

- The panel was transferred from wood to canvas in 1806 by the restorer François-Toussaint Hacquin, a brutal operation in which the original wood is planed away from the back.
- Art historians agree this version is entirely Leonardo's own hand. The London version involved the de Predis workshop.
- In 2011–2012 the two versions hung together at the National Gallery in London, possibly for the first time ever.
- A rock setting for a holy scene was close to unprecedented in 1483.

## Talking point

Stand back and cover the angel's pointing hand with your thumb. The whole composition loses its direction. That hand is doing most of the storytelling.
