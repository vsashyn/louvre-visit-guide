# The Virgin and Child in Majesty Surrounded by Angels (Maestà)

Cimabue, c. 1280. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 708 (Salon Carré).

## In one line

The start of the whole story. Everything else in the Italian galleries grows out of paintings like this one.

## What you are looking at

The Virgin enthroned, over four metres tall, holding the Christ child, with six angels stacked in two columns on either side. The ground is beaten gold. The drapery is picked out in fine gold lines, a Byzantine technique called chrysography.

Look at how the angels are arranged. They are simply placed one above the other, with no attempt at standing them in real space. Compare that to Giotto a few steps away and you can watch Western painting change.

## The story

Painted for the church of San Francesco in Pisa. Napoleon's armies took it, and it came to the Louvre in 1813, in the same seizure that brought Giotto's Saint Francis from the same church. Neither was returned.

## Look for

- The gold hatching on the Virgin's blue robe, drawn rather than modelled.
- The throne, which Cimabue attempts to give depth and does not quite manage.
- The scale. This is a monumental object made to dominate a church interior, and you are standing where only priests would have stood.

## Interesting facts

- Cimabue was, according to tradition, Giotto's teacher. The two paintings from the same Pisan church now hang in the same Paris room.
- This Maestà is stylistically earlier than the Santa Trinita Maestà in the Uffizi, painted without the pseudo-perspective of the later work.
- Cimabue's composition was copied widely, including by Duccio in the Rucellai Madonna, which was for centuries wrongly attributed to Cimabue himself.

## Talking point

Stand in front of this, then walk to the Giotto. Twenty years of art history in fifteen steps.
