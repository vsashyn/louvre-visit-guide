# The Battle of San Romano

Paolo Uccello, c. 1435–1455. Denon wing, Level 1, in the early Italian rooms.

## In one line

One third of a three-part battle scene, split between Paris, London and Florence, painted by a man obsessed with perspective to the point that Vasari said his wife could not get him to come to bed.

## What you are looking at

The Louvre panel shows the counterattack of Michelotto da Cotignola, the final episode of the 1432 battle between Florence and Siena. Horses in profile, lances at every angle, armour rendered in silver leaf.

Uccello was not painting a battle. He was using a battle as an excuse to solve perspective problems in public. Broken lances lie on the ground along the orthogonals, pointing to a vanishing point. Horses are foreshortened. Armour is drawn as intersecting solids.

## The story

The three panels were painted for a room in the Bartolini Salimbeni palace in Florence, designed to be hung high on three walls so the perspective would resolve. Lorenzo de' Medici wanted them badly. He bought one and had the other two removed from the palace by force.

Today: National Gallery in London, Uffizi in Florence, Louvre in Paris. Most historians put the narrative order as London, Uffizi, Louvre.

## Look for

- The silver leaf in the armour, now oxidised to grey and black. It was originally bright, and the panels would have glittered.
- The lances laid out on the ground like a drawing exercise.
- The rabbits, hounds and hunting figures in the background of the Louvre panel, going about their day behind a cavalry charge.
- The lack of blood. This is a heraldic, ceremonial view of war.

## Interesting facts

- All three panels have suffered from time and from early restoration, and none of them hang at the height Uccello designed for.
- The panels appeared in the 1968 portmanteau film Spirits of the Dead.
- The actual battle lasted six or seven hours and was a minor engagement. The paintings made it famous.

## Talking point

Find the vanishing point using the fallen lances. It is not where you expect, because the panel was meant to hang above your head.
