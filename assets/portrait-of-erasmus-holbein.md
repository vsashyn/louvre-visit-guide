# Portrait of Erasmus of Rotterdam Writing

Hans Holbein the Younger, 1523. Richelieu wing, Level 2, German painting rooms.

## In one line

The most influential intellectual in Europe, painted in profile, mid-sentence, by a 26-year-old who would go on to paint the English court.

## What you are looking at

Erasmus in profile, facing left, in a fur-collared coat and a scholar's cap. His hands rest on a book on a ledge in front of him, and he holds a quill. His eyes are down on the page. He is writing and you have caught him at it.

Holbein gives him no gaze, no engagement with the viewer, no rhetoric. A man at work.

## The story

Holbein painted this in Basel, where both men were living. Basel was a centre of humanist printing, and Erasmus was its most famous resident: the editor of the Greek New Testament, the author of In Praise of Folly, the leading voice of Christian humanism, and the man who tried and failed to hold a middle position as the Reformation split Europe.

Erasmus commissioned portraits from Holbein in 1523 to send to friends and patrons abroad, including Thomas More in England and Archbishop Warham. Several versions exist. When Holbein went to England in 1526, he carried a letter of introduction from Erasmus to More, which is how his English career began.

## Look for

- The book under his hands. An inscription partly in Greek runs around the fore-edges: The Labours of Hercules of Erasmus of Rotterdam. Erasmus used the phrase for his own workload, comparing his editing to the Twelve Labours.
- The quill and the specific grip.
- The hands. Holbein paints the veins and the knuckles of a man in his mid-fifties.
- The fur collar, and the difference in texture between fur, cloth and skin.

## Interesting facts

- Holbein painted Erasmus at least three times in 1523. Another version, showing him with his hands on a book, is in the National Gallery in London and one is at Longford Castle.
- Erasmus died in Basel in 1536, having declined both Luther's Reformation and Rome's demand that he denounce it.
- Holbein went to England, painted Thomas More, and later became Henry VIII's court painter. Both his great sitters, More and Erasmus, had been friends.

## Talking point

Erasmus commissioned this to send to people as a kind of calling card. It is a sixteenth-century author photo.
