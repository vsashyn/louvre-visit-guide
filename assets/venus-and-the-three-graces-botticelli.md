# Venus and the Three Graces Presenting Gifts to a Young Woman

Sandro Botticelli, c. 1483–1486. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 706, on the wall as you walk from the Winged Victory towards the Salon Carré.

## In one line

A wall from a Florentine villa, painted by Botticelli, found under whitewash in 1873 and cut off the building.

## What you are looking at

Venus, accompanied by the Three Graces, offers a gift to a young woman in contemporary Florentine dress. On the facing wall of the same room, and now hung nearby, a second fresco shows a young man presented by Prudence to Grammar and the Liberal Arts.

Botticelli emphasises the act of giving over whatever is being given. The gift itself, a cloth held in the Grace's hands, is almost invisible.

## The story

The frescoes decorated a first-floor room of the Villa Lemmi at Chiasso dei Macerelli near Florence, owned by the Tornabuoni family between 1469 and 1541. They were probably commissioned for the 1486 wedding of Lorenzo Tornabuoni to Giovanna degli Albizzi, and the two young figures are thought to be the couple.

They were found under whitewash during renovation in 1873, detached from the wall, and bought by the Louvre in 1882 from the dealer Stefano Bardini through Charles Ephrussi.

## Look for

- The damage. These were buried under lime for centuries and the surface is scarred. What survives is the drawing, which is Botticelli at his most fluent.
- The young woman's profile, in the Florentine portrait convention, against the mythological figures shown in three-quarter view.
- The bare feet and flowing drapery of the Graces, the same vocabulary as the Primavera in the Uffizi, painted in the same decade.

## Interesting facts

- Almost everyone walks straight past these on the way to the Mona Lisa. They are among the very few Botticelli frescoes outside Italy.
- Detaching a fresco, called strappo, pulls the pigment layer off the wall onto canvas. It is irreversible.
- The Louvre's official masterpieces trail stops here specifically because nobody stops here.

## Talking point

These were somebody's bedroom walls.
