# Sleeping Hermaphroditus

Roman, 2nd century AD, on a mattress carved by Bernini in 1620. Sully wing, Level 0, Room 348 (Salle des Caryatides).

## In one line

A joke that takes two walks around the sculpture to land.

## What you are looking at

From the doorway side, a woman asleep on her front, one leg drawn up, face turned into a pillow. Walk around to the other side and the body is that of Hermaphroditus, who in Ovid's telling was fused with the nymph Salmacis into a single being with both sexes.

The Louvre's own guide makes the point directly: this figure has to be seen from all sides to be understood. The Roman sculptor built the reveal into the staging.

## The story

Dug up in 1618 near Santa Maria della Vittoria in Rome, in the grounds of the ancient Gardens of Sallust. Cardinal Scipione Borghese took it for his villa and in 1620 paid the young Gian Lorenzo Bernini sixty scudi to carve the mattress.

The mattress is the other half of the trick. It is marble, and it sags. The buttoned ticking dimples under the weight of the hip, the corner of the pillow crumples. A twenty-two-year-old Bernini carved a soft thing out of stone and put it under a two-thousand-year-old body.

Prince Camillo Borghese, short of money and married to Pauline Bonaparte, sold it to France in 1807. It reached the Louvre in 1809.

## Look for

- The mattress, everywhere. The button, the seam, the dent under the knee.
- The two viewpoints, and the moment between them.
- The Salle des Caryatides itself, which is where you are standing. See its own file.

## Interesting facts

- The marble is thought to be a good early Imperial Roman copy of a bronze by the later of two Hellenistic sculptors named Polycles, working around 155 BC. Pliny mentions a famous bronze Hermaphroditus.
- At least five other ancient copies survive, including one in the Galleria Borghese found in 1781 and one in the Palazzo Massimo in Rome found in 1880.
- Velázquez commissioned a full-size bronze cast in 1652, and it fed into his Rokeby Venus.
- Swinburne wrote a poem called Hermaphroditus in 1863 after seeing this one.

## Talking point

Watch other visitors do the walk around. The reaction is the same every time and it is four hundred years old.
