# The Fortune Teller

Caravaggio, c. 1595–1598. Denon wing, Level 1, Grande Galerie, Italian seventeenth-century rooms.

## In one line

A boy is being robbed while he is being flirted with, and he has no idea.

## What you are looking at

A young gentleman in a plumed hat holds out his palm to a Romani woman who is reading it. They hold eye contact. Her fingers are stroking his hand. She is also slipping the ring off his finger.

Caravaggio has put the entire moral of the picture into a detail the size of a fingernail, and then made sure you look at the faces instead.

## The story

There are two versions. The first, around 1594, is in the Capitoline Museums in Rome, bought for eight scudi by the banker Vincenzo Giustiniani. This second version was painted for Cardinal Francesco Maria del Monte, Caravaggio's early patron.

The biographer Bellori claimed Caravaggio pulled a Romani girl in off the street to prove he did not need classical statues to paint from. The story is probably invented, but it says what contemporaries found shocking about him.

## Look for

- The ring, halfway off. Once you see it the painting changes completely.
- The background in this version: a real wall with the shadow of a half-drawn curtain and a window sash. The Roman version has a blank ground.
- His other hand on the sword hilt. He is armed and still being fleeced.

## Interesting facts

- This is one of only two genre scenes Caravaggio painted in this period. The other is the Cardsharps.
- In 1603 the poet Gaspare Murtola wrote a madrigal about it, saying the painter deceives the viewer exactly as the fortune teller deceives the boy.
- Caravaggio was in his early twenties. The dark theatrical style he is famous for came later.
