# The Coronation of Napoleon

Jacques-Louis David, 1805–1807. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 702 (Salle Daru).

## In one line

Ten metres of official history in which at least three important things did not happen the way they are shown.

## What you are looking at

Notre-Dame de Paris, 2 December 1804. Napoleon, already crowned, stands raising a second crown over the kneeling Joséphine. Behind him Pope Pius VII sits with his hand raised. Around them, roughly 150 identifiable figures: marshals, ministers, ambassadors, family.

David was Napoleon's First Painter. This is a state commission and it behaves like one.

## The story

Napoleon commissioned it orally in September 1804. David began on 21 December 1805 and finished in November 1807. He built a scale model of the cathedral interior with dressed dolls to work out the composition, and had participants sit for him individually.

The painting remained David's property until 1819, hung at Versailles from 1837 to 1889, then came to the Louvre.

### The things that did not happen

- David first painted Napoleon crowning himself. You can still make out the underlying design, arms raised over his own head. He changed it to the crowning of Joséphine, a gesture that is more gracious and less openly contemptuous of the Pope.
- Letizia Bonaparte, Napoleon's mother, sits in the centre of the tribune above, watching. She boycotted the ceremony over a quarrel between her sons and was not in the building.
- Pius VII was originally painted with his hands in his lap, doing nothing. Napoleon is said to have objected that the Pope should at least appear to be blessing, so David raised his hand. The Pope's role is reduced to a gesture.

## Look for

- David himself, in the tribune above and to the left of Letizia, sketching.
- Joséphine, painted far younger than her 41 years at Napoleon's instruction.
- The Ottoman ambassador Halet Efendi in the crowd.
- The sheer amount of red, gold and white velvet. David spent months on textiles.

## Interesting facts

- David painted a full-size second version, finished in 1822 during his exile in Brussels, largely from memory. It is at Versailles. The only deliberate change is that his favourite sister's dress is pink.
- Louis-Léopold Boilly painted the crowds looking at this painting at the Louvre in 1810.
- Ridley Scott restaged the composition in the 2023 film Napoleon, including a shot of David sketching.

## Talking point

Find the erased gesture. In raking light, the original arms-over-his-own-head pose is still faintly readable.
