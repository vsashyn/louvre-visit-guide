# Saint Francis of Assisi Receiving the Stigmata

Giotto, c. 1295–1300. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 708 (Salon Carré).

## In one line

The only signed Giotto panel outside Italy, and the moment painting started putting people in real space.

## What you are looking at

Francis kneels on the bare rock of Mount La Verna. A winged seraph in the form of the crucified Christ hovers above, and five thin golden rays connect the seraph's wounds to Francis's hands, feet and side. Below, a predella of three scenes: the dream of Innocent III, the pope approving the Franciscan rule, and Francis preaching to the birds.

Giotto gives the rocks weight. He gives Francis a body under the habit. The figures have volume and cast themselves into depth, which Cimabue's painting next door does not attempt.

## The story

Painted for the church of San Francesco in Pisa. Taken by Napoleon's armies and put on display in the Louvre in 1814, along with Cimabue's Maestà from the same church.

## Look for

- The signature. Along the bottom of the frame, in letters now dark against a darkened strip: OPUS IOCTI FLORENTINI, "the work of Giotto the Florentine".
- The five rays. Follow each one to its landing point.
- The little church on the left, drawn in a rough perspective that Giotto's generation was inventing as they went.
- The birds in the predella. One of the most reproduced images in medieval art, here at original size.

## Interesting facts

- The panel is 314 cm tall to the top of the gable.
- The stigmata episode was central to the Franciscans' argument that Francis was a second Christ, which is why it appears above the altar.
- Giotto's break with Byzantine convention is the single most-taught turning point in Western art history, and this panel is one of the exhibits.

## Talking point

Put your hand over the gold background. Under the gold, the rocks and the kneeling man would not look out of place three hundred years later.
