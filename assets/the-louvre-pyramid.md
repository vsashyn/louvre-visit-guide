# The Louvre Pyramid

I. M. Pei, 1984–1989. Cour Napoléon, the main entrance.

## In one line

A glass pyramid dropped into the courtyard of a Renaissance palace, hated on arrival, and now the second most recognisable thing about the museum.

## What you are looking at

A pyramid of glass and steel, 21.6 metres tall on a square base of 34 metres, in the middle of the Cour Napoléon. Three smaller pyramids flank it. The sides slope at 51.52 degrees, the same angle as the Egyptian pyramids at Giza.

It is a skylight. Everything it does is underneath it: the ticket hall, the entrances to the three wings, the shops, the auditoriums. Before 1989 the Louvre had no central entrance. Visitors came in through doors scattered around the perimeter and there was nowhere to buy a ticket, leave a coat or find a lavatory.

## The story

François Mitterrand selected Pei in 1983 without a competition, which was itself controversial. Pei designed it in late 1983, presented it in early 1984, and it opened in 1989.

The objections came in four kinds: it clashed with the Renaissance and Baroque architecture, a pyramid is a tomb and therefore morbid, it was a presidential vanity project, and Pei was Chinese-American and therefore, some argued, unqualified to touch a French monument. That last argument was made in print by people who should have known better.

Pei's answer to the first objection is the glass. He specified the clearest glass available, made to order, so that the palace facades remain visible through the structure from every angle.

### The 666 myth

A persistent story says the pyramid has exactly 666 panes, the number of the beast. It is false.

There are 673 panes: 603 rhombuses and 70 triangles. The 666 figure appeared twice in official brochures during construction and never went away. Pei's office has said Mitterrand never specified any number of panes. Dan Brown recycled the story in The Da Vinci Code, which guaranteed it will never die.

## Look for

- The reflections. The pyramid mirrors the palace, so the courtyard facades appear twice.
- The steel cable structure inside, which is a piece of engineering worth looking up at from the ticket hall.
- The three small pyramids and the triangular pools.
- The Inverted Pyramid, in the Carrousel du Louvre shopping arcade to the west, hanging point-down as a skylight over the underground passage.

## Interesting facts

- The project, the Grand Louvre, also cleared the Ministry of Finance out of the Richelieu wing, which nearly doubled the museum's exhibition space.
- Pei was 66 when he was appointed and 71 when it opened. He died in 2019 at 102.
- The pyramid took two years of hostile press and then, roughly at the moment it opened, the argument stopped.

## Practical

The Pyramid entrance has the longest queues. The Carrousel du Louvre entrance underneath, reached from 99 rue de Rivoli or the Palais Royal Musée du Louvre metro, is usually much faster. The Porte des Lions entrance opens irregularly. Book a timed ticket online regardless.

## Talking point

Find the angle in the courtyard where the pyramid's reflection lines up exactly with the real facade behind it.
