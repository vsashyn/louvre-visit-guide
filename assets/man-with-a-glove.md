# Man with a Glove

Titian, c. 1520–1523. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 711 (Salle des États).

## In one line

An unidentified young man, painted so well that four centuries of novelists have used him as shorthand for European civilisation.

## What you are looking at

A young man of about twenty against a flat black ground. White shirt, dark doublet, a gold ring and a chain with a sapphire and a pearl. His gloved left hand holds a second glove. His bare right hand rests on a stone parapet, relaxed and slightly forward.

Titian gives you almost nothing except the face, the hands and the whites. That is the whole picture.

## The story

The sitter is unknown. Candidates include Girolamo Adorno, mentioned in a 1527 letter from Pietro Aretino, the Gonzaga agent Giambattista Malatesta, and Ferrante Gonzaga, who would have been sixteen in 1523.

Provenance: Gonzaga collection in Mantua, Charles I of England in 1627, sold after his execution in 1649 to Eberhard Jabach, then to Louis XIV, then to the Louvre in 1792. The same route as the Pastoral Concert hanging near it.

## Look for

- The two hands doing different jobs. One gloved and formal, one bare and casual.
- The parapet, Titian's standard device in his early portraits.
- How much of the canvas is simply black.

## Interesting facts

- Henry James used the painting in The Ambassadors in 1903, where a character is struck by "the overwhelming portrait".
- Albert Camus put a reproduction of it on a character's wall in A Happy Death.
- Titian was in his early thirties. This is the portrait style he would refine for the next fifty years.

## Talking point

Age him. Most people guess between eighteen and twenty-five, and the clothes and jewellery are the only hard evidence.
