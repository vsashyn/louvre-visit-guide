# The Slaughtered Ox

Rembrandt van Rijn, 1655. Richelieu wing, Level 2, Room 844.

## In one line

A carcass hanging in a cellar, and it runs a straight line from 1655 to Soutine and Francis Bacon.

## What you are looking at

A flayed ox strung up on a wooden frame in a dark room, split open, the ribcage and the fat and the meat lit from somewhere to the left. In the shadow at the back, a woman's head appears in a doorway, easy to miss.

The paint is heaped on. Rembrandt built the fat and muscle in thick impasto, so that the surface of the painting is physically the texture of the thing it depicts.

## The story

In northern Europe, November was slaughtering month. Animals that could not be fed through winter were killed and salted, and a hanging carcass was a sign of a household with supplies. It was a recognisable domestic subject, not a shock image.

Rembrandt was 49 and two years from bankruptcy. The painting is on beech panel, unusual for him.

Joshua Reynolds saw it in Amsterdam in 1781. Louis Viardot sold it to the Louvre in 1857 for 5,000 francs.

## Look for

- The head in the doorway at the back left, watching.
- The impasto. Stand at an angle to the surface and you can see the paint standing proud.
- The wooden frame, roughly nailed, which is the only straight line in the picture.
- The colour: cream, ochre, red-brown, and the strange blue-white of the bone.

## Interesting facts

- Chaïm Soutine painted the subject obsessively in the 1920s, hanging real carcasses in his studio until neighbours called the police over the smell. Carcass of Beef of 1925 is the direct descendant.
- Francis Bacon put a version of it behind the screaming pope in Figure with Meat in 1954.
- Delacroix and Daumier both drew from it.
- Similar paintings in Glasgow, Budapest and Philadelphia are now generally given to pupils.
- Scholars argue about whether it carries vanitas meaning or refers to the Prodigal Son, who ends up among the animals. It may just be a carcass.

## Talking point

It is the least flattering subject Rembrandt could have chosen and it is one of the most physically beautiful paint surfaces in the museum.
