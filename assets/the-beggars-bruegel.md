# The Beggars

Pieter Bruegel the Elder, 1568. Richelieu wing, Level 2, Flemish painting rooms.

## In one line

The only Bruegel in the Louvre, it is 18 by 21 centimetres, and everyone walks past it.

## What you are looking at

Five disabled men on crutches in a courtyard outside a hospital or almshouse wall. A sixth figure, a woman, walks away at the back carrying a bowl. Behind them, through an arch, a green garden and water.

Each of the five wears a different hat: a cardboard crown, a paper military shako, a bourgeois beret, a peasant's cap, a bishop's mitre. The five estates of society, all crippled, all begging.

Fox tails are pinned to their coats.

## The story

The fox tails have been connected to the Geuzen, the "beggars", the Dutch nobles who took that name as a badge after a Habsburg official dismissed them as beggars in 1566. They wore begging bowls and fox tails as insignia during the revolt against Philip II of Spain.

That reading is tempting and it may be wrong. Bruegel had already used fox tails in The Fight Between Carnival and Lent in 1559, well before the Geuzen existed. The tails may simply be what beggars wore.

Another reading takes the picture literally: these are lepers or victims of ergotism outside a hospital, and the hats are carnival costume worn on a specific feast day when the disabled were permitted to beg publicly.

## Look for

- The five hats, one per social estate.
- The legs. Bruegel draws the specific mechanics of amputation and crutch use.
- The woman walking away, the only person leaving.
- The green garden through the arch, painted with real delicacy, which has nothing to do with the foreground.

## Interesting facts

- Two sixteenth-century inscriptions are on the back: a fragmentary Flemish text and a Latin verse praising Bruegel for surpassing nature.
- It was painted in 1568, the year the Dutch Revolt turned into open war. Bruegel died the following year.
- It came to the Louvre as a gift in 1892.
- Sixteenth-century Europeans generally showed very little sympathy for beggars, and the composition, with the figures positioned outside the walls, reflects that.

## Talking point

It is smaller than a postcard. Stand close and count the hats before you read the label.
