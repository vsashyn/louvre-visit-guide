# The Salon Carré

Denon wing, Level 1, Room 708.

## In one line

The room that gave the world the word "Salon".

## What you are looking at

A square room between the Botticelli frescoes and the Grande Galerie, now hung with early Italian painting from the twelfth to the fifteenth century, including Cimabue's Maestà and Giotto's Saint Francis.

## The story

In the eighteenth century this room was the exhibition space for members of the Royal Academy of Painting and Sculpture. The Academy held public displays here, and they were named after the room: the Salon.

The Salons ran through the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries and were the central institution of French art. Living artists exhibited, the public came in large numbers, and art criticism as a form of writing was invented in response, starting with Diderot's reviews from 1759.

Reputations were made and destroyed here. Géricault showed The Raft of the Medusa at the Salon of 1819, Delacroix showed Sardanapalus in 1827, and both were savaged. The refusals mattered as much as the acceptances. By 1863 the rejections were so numerous and so contested that Napoleon III ordered a parallel Salon des Refusés, where Manet showed Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe.

### How the pictures were hung

Frame to frame, floor to ceiling, covering every inch of wall. Small pictures went at eye level, large pictures high up and tilted forward. Being "skied", hung near the ceiling, could kill a painting's reception.

Giuseppe Castiglione's 1861 painting of the Salon Carré, in the Louvre's collection, shows exactly how dense it was.

### What happened to it

The Salon exhibitions moved out of the Louvre in 1848 to give the museum more room. The Salon Carré was then reserved for outstanding individual paintings, and the Mona Lisa hung here for a period. The dense hang gave way to the modern practice of a single line of pictures with space around them.

## Look for

- The square proportions, which is where the name comes from.
- Cimabue's Maestà and Giotto's Saint Francis, both taken from the same church in Pisa by Napoleon's armies.
- The height of the walls, and how much more could be hung on them than is hung now.
- The ceiling and cornice, remodelled in the nineteenth century when the room was given over to the museum.

## Interesting facts

- The word Salon, for an art exhibition, comes from this room.
- Diderot invented art criticism as a literary form writing about the Salons held here, starting in 1759.
- Paintings were hung frame to frame from floor to ceiling. Being hung near the ceiling, called being skied, could sink a painting's reception.
- Giuseppe Castiglione's 1861 painting of this room, in the Louvre's collection, records exactly how dense the hang was.
- The Salon exhibitions left the Louvre in 1848. The Mona Lisa hung here for a period afterwards.

## Talking point

Every art exhibition you have ever been to is descended from what happened in this room.
