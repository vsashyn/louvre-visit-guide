# Milo of Croton

Pierre Puget, 1671–1682. Richelieu wing, Level -1, Room 105, the Cour Puget.

## In one line

"The poor man, how he suffers." Queen Marie-Thérèse, on first seeing it at Versailles.

## What you are looking at

A powerfully built older man, naked, with his right hand caught in the split of a tree stump. He twists away, his head thrown back, his mouth open. A lion has its teeth in his left thigh and is pulling. A victory cup lies on the ground at his feet.

Everything in the sculpture is torsion. The trapped hand fixes one end of the body, the lion pulls the other, and the whole figure wrenches between them.

## The story

Milo of Croton was a real athlete of the sixth century BC, an Olympic wrestling champion six times over and the most famous strongman of antiquity. In old age, according to Pausanias, he tried to prove his strength had not left him by splitting a tree trunk with his bare hands. The wedge fell out, the trunk closed on his hand, and he was eaten alive by wild animals.

Ancient sources differ on what ate him. Pausanias says wolves. Puget carved a lion, which is the version that entered the later tradition.

Puget worked in Toulon and Marseille, far from the court, and had a reputation as a difficult man. He delivered the group to Versailles in 1683. After what contemporaries described as a moment of hesitation in the face of its violence, Louis XIV approved it and gave it a place of honour on the edge of the Tapis Vert, the great lawn.

## Look for

- The trapped hand. Find it, in the cleft of the stump. It is the pivot of the whole design.
- The cup on the ground, the prize from an athletic contest, spilled and useless.
- The face. Puget carves physical pain and the collapse of self-image in the same expression.
- The lion's grip, and the way the flesh of the thigh gives under the teeth.

## Interesting facts

- It is generally treated as the defining work of French Baroque sculpture, far removed from the polite classicism that dominated Versailles.
- A copy now stands in the gardens at Versailles.
- Puget was a painter, sculptor, architect and ship-decorator. He carved the ornamentation on French naval vessels at Toulon.
- Bronze reductions of the group were popular for two centuries.

## Talking point

Everything around it in the Cour Puget is composed and dignified. Ask how this got past the king.
