# The Astronomer

Johannes Vermeer, 1668. Richelieu wing, Level 2, Room 837, beside The Lacemaker.

## In one line

A man reaching for a globe in a shaft of window light, with a small swastika stamped in black ink on the back of the canvas.

## What you are looking at

A scholar in a loose blue-grey robe sits at a table by a window, one hand reaching out to turn a celestial globe. A book lies open in front of him. On the wall behind, a painting of the finding of Moses. A cupboard, an astrolabe, a carpet on the table.

Everything in the picture leans toward the globe. The light, the hand, the fold of the robe, the diagonal of the table.

### The objects, identified

Vermeer painted real instruments accurately enough that they can be named.

- The celestial globe is by Jodocus Hondius, and the constellations on it can be matched to a specific edition.
- The book is open at Book III of Adriaan Metius's Institutiones Astronomicae Geographicae, in the 1621 edition. Book III advises the astronomer to seek inspiration from God. Vermeer chose that page.
- No telescope. The astronomer works from a globe and a book, which is how most of the discipline was done.

## The story

The Rothschild family owned it. In 1940 the Einsatzstab Reichsleiter Rosenberg, the Nazi art-looting unit, seized it from Édouard de Rothschild's collection. It was earmarked for the Führermuseum that Hitler planned for Linz. Someone stamped a small black swastika on the back of the canvas to mark it as Reich property. The stamp is still there.

It was recovered after the war and returned to the Rothschilds. In 1983 the family gave it to the French state in lieu of inheritance tax, and it went to the Louvre.

## Look for

- The hand on the globe. Vermeer paints the pressure of fingertips on a curved surface.
- The light through the leaded window, and the way the panes are drawn as loose highlights.
- The painting-within-the-painting on the back wall, the finding of Moses, which also appears in Vermeer's The Love Letter.
- The signature and date on the cupboard: IVMeer MDCLXVIII.

## Interesting facts

- The Geographer, now in the Städel in Frankfurt, is its companion. Same model, same robe, same room, painted the same year, on canvas from the same bolt. The two were reunited in an exhibition in 1997 for the first time since 1797.
- The model may be Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, the Delft microscopist, who was the same age as Vermeer, lived in the same town and was appointed executor of Vermeer's estate. It is unproven.
- Vermeer's paintings hung in ordinary Delft houses. The idea that he was a forgotten genius is exaggerated, but he was certainly rediscovered in the nineteenth century.

## Talking point

The swastika on the back is not on display and never will be. Ask whether it should be.
