# Plaque of the Ergastines

Athens, 445–438 BC. Sully wing, Level 0, Room 347.

## In one line

A slab of the Parthenon, in Paris, and nobody argues about it because everyone is arguing about the ones in London.

## What you are looking at

Eight figures from the east frieze of the Parthenon. Six young women, the ergastines, walk in procession, and two officials turn to receive them. The women wove the peplos, the new robe offered to Athena at the Great Panathenaia, the festival held every four years.

The carving is high Classical at its peak. The relief is shallow, no more than about six centimetres deep, and inside that depth the sculptors produce overlapping bodies, fabric that reveals the legs beneath, and individual knuckles.

## The story

The frieze ran 160 metres around the outside of the Parthenon's inner building, forty feet above the ground and visible only in slices between the columns.

This block was found at the foot of the Parthenon by the French antiquarian Louis-François-Sébastien Fauvel and bought by the Comte de Choiseul-Gouffier, French ambassador to the Ottoman court, in 1784. When Choiseul-Gouffier emigrated during the Revolution his property was confiscated, and the block came to the Louvre in 1798. This is a generation before Lord Elgin.

## Look for

- The drapery. Vertical falls of cloth read as fluting, echoing the columns of the building itself.
- The hands. Each woman holds a different attribute, and several hands would originally have held bronze objects now lost.
- The two officials, turning back to speak, breaking the rhythm of the procession.
- The surface. It was painted and had metal fittings. What you see is the underlying stone.

## Interesting facts

- The Louvre holds one full block of the frieze plus a fragment. The Acropolis Museum in Athens and the British Museum hold the bulk of what survives.
- Greece's repatriation campaign focuses overwhelmingly on the British Museum's holdings. The French block is far less discussed.
- The Parthenon was built between 447 and 432 BC, which means the frieze was carved in under ten years by a workshop of many hands under Phidias.

## Talking point

The frieze was designed to be seen from forty feet below, through a colonnade. You are looking at it at eye level from a metre away, which nobody in antiquity ever did.
