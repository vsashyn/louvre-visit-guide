# The Galerie d'Apollon

Le Vau and Le Brun, 1661–1663, completed 1851. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 705, next to the Winged Victory.

## In one line

The room where Louis XIV invented himself as the Sun King, and the prototype for the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles.

## What you are looking at

A gallery 61 metres long, barrel-vaulted, encrusted from floor to ceiling in gilded stucco, painted panels, marble and carved wood. The programme is the sun: Apollo's chariot, the hours of the day, the months, the seasons, the signs of the zodiac, the four continents. Light moving through time and space, with Apollo at the centre, and Apollo is the king.

## The story

The Petite Galerie burned on 6 February 1661. Louis XIV, then 22 and newly in personal control of the state, ordered it rebuilt. Louis Le Vau handled the architecture between 1661 and 1663. Charles Le Brun designed the entire decorative scheme. François Girardon and the Marsy brothers made the stucco sculpture.

Le Brun's Apollo programme here is the trial run. He went on to do the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles with the same idea at greater scale.

The gallery was left unfinished when Louis moved the court to Versailles in the 1670s. Félix Duban restored it in the nineteenth century, and Eugène Delacroix painted the central ceiling panel, Apollo Slaying the Python, in 1851. Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte inaugurated the completed gallery on 5 June 1851, 190 years after the fire.

### What is not there any more

From 1887 the gallery housed the remaining French Crown Jewels, including the Regent diamond of about 140 carats, the Hortensia, and the Sancy.

On 19 October 2025, four thieves dressed as construction workers used a furniture lift to reach a window on the Seine side, cut in, and took eight pieces in under eight minutes. The stolen items were valued at around 88 million euros. Empress Eugénie's crown was dropped during the escape and recovered outside, badly deformed but nearly intact, and is being restored. The rest is still missing.

The gallery reopened on 22 July 2026 with the display cases removed and no jewels in it. The Louvre has said the surviving jewels will be shown in a separate, windowless strongroom gallery.

## Look for

- Delacroix's Apollo Slaying the Python in the centre of the vault. It is a Romantic painting inserted into a Baroque scheme and it works.
- The gilded stucco figures in the coving.
- The zodiac and the months along the sides.
- The floor, and the length of the sightline down the gallery.

## Interesting facts

- Le Brun's scheme was the direct model for the Hall of Mirrors.
- The gallery took 190 years to finish.
- Before the jewels arrived in 1887 it had been used to display drawings and other collections.

## Practical

The gallery reopened on 22 July 2026 with the cases removed and no jewels in it, so this is now an architecture visit. It sits directly beside the Winged Victory at the top of the Daru staircase, which makes it a natural two-minute detour. Confirm it is open on the day, because security arrangements here are still changing.

## Talking point

It reopened as a room with nothing in it, and the room was always the point.
