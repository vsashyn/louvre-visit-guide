# Portrait of Louis XIV

Hyacinthe Rigaud, 1701. Sully wing, Level 2, French seventeenth-century painting.

## In one line

The definitive image of absolute monarchy, painted as a present for the king's grandson, and the king liked it so much he kept it and sent the grandson a copy.

## What you are looking at

Louis XIV, 62, in coronation robes: blue velvet strewn with gold fleurs-de-lis, lined with ermine, thrown back off one shoulder. He stands with one hand on his hip and the other on a sceptre reversed like a walking stick. The crown sits on a cushion beside him. His legs are bare to the knee in white stockings and red-heeled shoes, one foot turned out in a dancer's fourth position.

Behind him, a column, a throne with fleur-de-lis embroidery, and a great swag of red curtain. Reliefs of Justice and Strength are carved on the column base.

## The story

Charles II of Spain died childless in 1700 and left the Spanish throne to Louis XIV's grandson Philippe, Duke of Anjou. Before Philippe left to become Philip V of Spain, he asked for a portrait of his grandfather.

Rigaud delivered. Louis thought the result so successful that he kept the original and had Rigaud produce a copy for Madrid. It became the official state portrait, reproduced in engravings by Pierre Drevet and copied endlessly for embassies and public buildings.

## Look for

- The Sword of Charlemagne, called Joyeuse, at his left hip, the coronation sword of the kings of France. It is in the Louvre's decorative arts collection.
- The sceptre, held upside down like a cane. The gesture is casual and the object is not.
- The legs. Louis had been a celebrated dancer in his youth and was proud of them at 62. He insisted the portrait be truthful about his face and flattering about his legs, and Rigaud managed both.
- The wig, an enormous black leonine mass. Louis was bald.
- The face. It is the face of an old man, and Rigaud did not soften it.

## Interesting facts

- Rigaud painted the head separately on a small canvas and had it stitched into the large one, a standard workshop practice for grand portraits.
- Versions and replicas are at Versailles, the Getty in Los Angeles, and the Palais Rohan in Strasbourg.
- Louis XIV reigned for 72 years, the longest of any European sovereign. He had fourteen years left when this was painted.
- The War of the Spanish Succession, caused by the same inheritance that prompted the portrait, broke out the same year and ran until 1714.

## Talking point

He is dressed for his coronation, which took place in 1654, forty-seven years before this was painted, when he was fifteen.
