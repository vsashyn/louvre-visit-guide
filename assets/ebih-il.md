# Statue of Ebih-Il, Superintendent of Mari

Sumerian, c. 2400 BC. Richelieu wing, Level 0, Room 234.

## In one line

A 4,400-year-old civil servant with lapis lazuli eyes and an unmistakable small smile.

## What you are looking at

A man sits on a woven hassock, bare to the waist, wearing a kaunakes, the tufted fleece skirt of Sumerian dress, with the animal's tail still attached at the back. His hands are clasped against his chest in the standard Mesopotamian gesture of prayer. He is bald and clean-shaven.

The eyes are the reason to stop. Schist forms the eyelashes and lids, shell the whites, lapis lazuli the irises. Against the pale gypsum the blue is startling.

## The story

André Parrot's French excavations at Mari, modern Tell Hariri in eastern Syria on the Euphrates, found him in the Temple of Ishtar: the head on 22 January 1934, the body the next day.

An inscription on his shoulder identifies him: Ebih-Il, nu-banda, dedicated to Ishtar Virile. A nu-banda was a superintendent, a senior administrative and military post.

Statues like this were votive. You placed your own image in the temple so that it could go on praying on your behalf, permanently, while you got on with your job. Ebih-Il has been praying for 4,400 years.

## Look for

- The eyes, from the front and slightly to the side.
- The smile. It is barely there and it changes the whole face.
- The kaunakes. Each tuft is individually carved.
- The inscription on the right shoulder.
- The hands, one clasped over the other.

## Interesting facts

- The lapis lazuli came from Badakhshan in what is now Afghanistan, roughly 4,000 km away. Its presence in a Syrian temple statue is direct evidence of long-range Bronze Age trade.
- Mari was a major city controlling Euphrates trade. Its royal archive of some 20,000 clay tablets is one of the richest sources for Bronze Age Near Eastern history.
- Tell Hariri was looted during the Syrian civil war.
- Statues of this type are generally called "worshipper figures". The Louvre's is the finest surviving example.

## Talking point

He was made to pray forever on someone's behalf. He is now doing it in a museum in Paris, which is not what anyone had in mind.
