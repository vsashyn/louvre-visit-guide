# Portrait of the Artist Holding a Thistle

Albrecht Dürer, 1493. Richelieu wing, Level 2, in the German painting rooms.

## In one line

A 22-year-old paints himself, alone, for no client, which almost nobody in Europe had done before.

## What you are looking at

A young man in a grey-violet doublet with a scarlet tasselled cap and a shirt gathered at the neck with a cord. His hair falls in long waves. He holds a small spray of blue-flowered plant between his fingers, presenting it toward you.

Above his head, an inscription in German: "My affairs will go as ordained on high." The date, 1493, is next to it.

## The story

Self-portraits existed before this, but almost always as a face in a crowd scene, or a reflection in an object. An independent painted self-portrait, made as its own subject, is close to unprecedented in Northern Europe in 1493.

Dürer did it three times: this one, the Prado self-portrait of 1498, and the Munich self-portrait of 1500 in which he paints himself frontally in the pose of Christ.

### The thistle

Botanists identify the plant as Eryngium amethystinum, sea holly. In fifteenth-century German the plant was called Mannstreu, "man's fidelity", and it symbolised conjugal faithfulness.

That gives the painting a job. Dürer was in Strasbourg in 1493, at the end of his journeyman years. His father was arranging his marriage. He married Agnes Frey on 7 July 1494, a few months after this was painted, having probably not met her. The painting was very likely sent home as a betrothal portrait, a picture of the man Agnes was going to marry, holding a promise of fidelity.

The plant also has a Christian reading, as a symbol of the Passion, and the inscription is fatalistic. Both readings can be true at once.

## Look for

- The hands. Dürer's hands are unusually prominent in all three self-portraits.
- The support. It is painted on vellum, which is extremely rare for a panel-sized portrait and suggests it was made to be rolled and sent.
- The inscription, in German rather than Latin.
- The cord at the neck of the shirt.

## Interesting facts

- Dürer was 22. He had finished his apprenticeship with Michael Wolgemut and his journeyman travels, and had not yet been to Italy.
- The vellum was mounted onto canvas around 1840, which is why the surface looks unlike a panel painting.
- The Louvre bought it in 1922.
- Dürer's 1500 Munich self-portrait, painting himself in the frontal pose reserved for Christ, would have been close to blasphemous in almost anyone else's hands.

## Talking point

He painted this before he had met the woman it was probably painted for.
