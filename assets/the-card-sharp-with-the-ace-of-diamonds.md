# The Card Sharp with the Ace of Diamonds

Georges de La Tour, c. 1636–1638. Sully wing, Level 2, French seventeenth-century painting.

## In one line

Four people, three of whom are in on it, and the fourth is the one with money.

## What you are looking at

A card table. On the right, a richly dressed young man with a plumed hat and a pile of gold coins, looking at his own cards. In the centre, a courtesan in a gold and black dress, turning her head to the left with her eyes cut sideways. Behind her, a maid in a turban leaning in with a glass of wine. On the left, a man in profile reaching behind his back to pull the ace of diamonds from his belt.

The three conspirators are connected by a chain of glances. The young man is connected to nobody.

## The story

Seventeenth-century moralists warned young men against three temptations: wine, gambling and lust. La Tour puts all three at one table, in that order, left to right, and makes them beautiful.

The Louvre bought it in 1972. La Tour had been completely forgotten for two and a half centuries and was only rediscovered in 1915, which is why a painter of this quality was still available on the market in the 1970s.

## Look for

- The chain of glances. Courtesan to maid, maid to card sharp, card sharp out toward you. Follow it in order.
- The ace of diamonds, coming out from behind his belt.
- The signature, in the shadow of the tablecloth under the card sharp's elbow: Georgius De La Tour fecit.
- The textures. La Tour paints gold braid, pearl, feather, wine and satin as four completely different substances.
- The young man's face, which is the only relaxed one in the picture.

## Interesting facts

- There are two versions. The other, The Cheat with the Ace of Clubs, is in the Kimbell Art Museum in Fort Worth, bought in 1981. They differ in colour, clothing and detail, and scholars argue about which came first.
- La Tour is now known mostly for his night scenes lit by a single candle. This is a daylight painting and shows a different side of him.
- He was rediscovered when Hermann Voss published three paintings under his name in 1915. Before that, his works were attributed to Vermeer, Velázquez, Zurbarán and others.

## Talking point

The young man is the only person in the painting you can see clearly. Ask why La Tour lit the mark and shaded the thieves.
