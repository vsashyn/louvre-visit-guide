# The Salle des Caryatides

Pierre Lescot and Jean Goujon, 1546–1550. Sully wing, Level 0, Room 348.

## In one line

France's first Renaissance ballroom, the site of a royal wedding that ended in a massacre, now full of Roman statues.

## What you are looking at

A long hall on the ground floor of the Lescot wing, the oldest standing part of the Louvre. At the far end, a musicians' gallery is carried on four colossal female figures carved by Jean Goujon, the caryatids that give the room its name.

Caryatids, women used as columns, come from the Erechtheion on the Athenian Acropolis. Lescot and Goujon used them here to cast Henri II as a Roman emperor, as Augustus rebuilding Rome. The whole room is an argument that the French monarchy is the heir of antiquity.

At the other end, a monumental fireplace.

## The story

The room was a ballroom and a court of justice.

In August 1572, Marguerite de Valois, Queen Margot, married Henri de Navarre here. The marriage was meant to reconcile Catholics and Protestants. Protestant nobles came to Paris in large numbers for the wedding.

A few days later, on 24 August, the Saint Bartholomew's Day Massacre began. Protestant nobles who had come for the wedding were killed in and around the Louvre. The killing spread through Paris and then through France, and the death toll ran into the thousands.

Molière's troupe performed in this room before Louis XIV.

### What is in it now

From 1692 the room was used to display the royal collection of classical sculpture, and it still is. The Sleeping Hermaphroditus, bought by Napoleon in 1807, is here. So is the Diana the Huntress, one of the first antiquities to enter the French royal collection.

## Look for

- The four caryatids and the gallery they carry.
- The fireplace at the opposite end.
- The Sleeping Hermaphroditus, and walk around it.
- The proportions. This is what a French room looked like when Italian ideas first arrived.

## Interesting facts

- Jean Goujon is the outstanding French sculptor of the sixteenth century. He also carved the Fountain of the Innocents, in the 1st arrondissement, a short walk from here.
- The Lescot wing is the west range of the Cour Carrée, begun in 1546 when Francis I pulled down the medieval keep.
- The Louvre's official masterpieces trail starts in this room.

## Talking point

The wedding that was meant to end the French wars of religion was held here, and it triggered the worst episode of them.
