# The Madonna of Chancellor Rolin

Jan van Eyck, c. 1435. Richelieu wing, Level 2, in the early Netherlandish rooms.

## In one line

Sixty-six centimetres wide, and there is a bridge in it with individual people walking across it.

## What you are looking at

Nicolas Rolin, Chancellor of Burgundy, kneels at a prayer desk in a marble loggia, facing the Virgin, who holds the Christ child while an angel lowers a crown onto her head. Between them, three round-arched openings give onto a garden, a river, a bridge and a city that runs back to blue mountains.

Van Eyck painted in oil with layered translucent glazes over a white ground, which is why the light seems to come from inside the panel rather than off it. Nobody in Europe could do this in 1435 except him.

## The story

Nicolas Rolin was about sixty. He had risen from provincial lawyer to the most powerful administrator in Burgundy, and he was not popular. Contemporaries accused him of enriching himself. He commissioned this for his parish church, Notre-Dame-du-Chastel in Autun.

The church burned in 1793. The panel survived, went to Autun cathedral, and came to the Louvre in 1805.

## Look for

- The two men on the parapet in the middle distance, leaning over the crenellation with their backs to you, looking at the view. One wears a red chaperon. They may be van Eyck and an assistant.
- The city. Around the bridge there are boats, a mill, traders, tiny figures on the quay. It is usually read as Autun, or as an invented composite, or as the heavenly Jerusalem.
- The carved capitals in the loggia, which show Old Testament scenes: the expulsion from Eden, Noah's drunkenness, Cain and Abel. They are on Rolin's side only.
- The tiled floor, which runs in exact perspective.
- The peacocks and magpies in the garden.
- The jewels on the angel's cope and on the crown, each one painted with a separate highlight.

## Interesting facts

- Infrared reflectography found that Rolin originally had a large purse hanging from his belt. It was painted out, presumably because his wealth was already the problem.
- The infant Christ was changed from pointing downward to raising his hand in blessing.
- The Virgin and Rolin are the same size, on the same level, in the same space. In 1435 that was a startling claim for a layman to make.
- Five of the seven deadly sins have been identified in the carvings and animals, all on Rolin's side.
- The panel was restored in 2023–2024 ahead of the Louvre's Jan van Eyck exhibition.

## Practical

Bring a phone with a good zoom, or use the museum's own high-resolution image later. This is a painting that rewards magnification more than any other in the building.

## Talking point

Find the two men on the bridge parapet. Then ask why van Eyck put spectators inside his own painting looking at the view instead of at the Virgin.
