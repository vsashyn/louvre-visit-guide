# The Marie de' Medici Cycle

Peter Paul Rubens, 1622–1625. Richelieu wing, Level 2, Room 801, the Galerie Médicis.

## In one line

Twenty-four enormous canvases in a purpose-built room, commissioned by a queen to rewrite her own catastrophic political record as mythology, and almost nobody goes in.

## What you are looking at

A single room, close to 300 square metres of painting. Twenty-one canvases narrate the life of Marie de' Medici, plus three portraits of her and her parents. They are hung clockwise in chronological order, as they were designed to be.

Every episode is staged with gods. Her birth is attended by the Fates. Her education is supervised by Minerva, Apollo and Mercury. Her arrival in Marseille is met by Neptune and a trio of naiads who are among the most physical figures Rubens ever painted.

## The story

Henri IV was assassinated in 1610 and Marie became regent for the eight-year-old Louis XIII. She kept ruling after he came of age at thirteen. In 1617, aged fifteen, Louis had her favourite Concini murdered and exiled his mother to Blois. She escaped, raised an army against her son, lost, and was reconciled in 1621.

She commissioned the cycle for her new Luxembourg Palace in autumn 1621, with the contract settled in early 1622 and the work delivered by the end of 1624. It hung in her reception gallery, which means visitors waiting to see her were walked through her version of events.

A companion cycle on the life of Henri IV was planned for the facing gallery. Marie was banished again in 1631 after losing a final power struggle with Richelieu, and it was abandoned. Nine oil sketches and five unfinished canvases survive.

## Look for

- The Disembarkation at Marseille. The three naiads in the foreground are the passage everyone photographs.
- The Presentation of the Portrait. Henri IV is shown Marie's portrait by Cupid and Hymen while Jupiter and Juno look on. A political marriage negotiated to clear Henri's debts to the Medici bank, rendered as a love story.
- The Coronation in Saint-Denis. The one event in the cycle that needed no embellishment.
- The Death of Henri IV and the Proclamation of the Regency. The assassination is pushed to one side and the regency dominates.
- The Felicity of the Regency. Rubens painted this on the spot in Paris to replace a canvas showing Marie's 1617 expulsion, which everyone agreed was too dangerous.

## Interesting facts

- Rubens painted himself into the proxy wedding scene, standing behind the bride holding a cross and looking out at you.
- The cycle cost roughly 24,000 guilders, about 82 guilders per square metre.
- Cézanne applied ten separate times for permission to copy the figure of Bellona from the death of Henri IV.
- Rubens was a working diplomat as well as a painter, which is part of why he was trusted with a commission this politically delicate.

## Practical

This is one of the great rooms of the museum and it is usually close to empty. Allow twenty-five minutes and walk it clockwise.

## Talking point

Pick the single least honest painting in the room. There is real competition.
