# Sarcophagus of the Spouses

Etruscan, c. 520–510 BC, from Cerveteri. Denon wing, Level 0, Etruscan galleries.

## In one line

A married couple at dinner, on their own coffin, looking delighted about it.

## What you are looking at

A man and a woman recline together on a kline, a dining couch, propped on cushions. He is bare-chested with his arm around her shoulders. She is fully dressed, wearing soft pointed shoes and a cap. Both are smiling the archaic smile. Their hands are held out in gestures that once held objects, probably a perfume flask and an egg.

It is a terracotta urn for ashes. The couple are sharing eternity the way Etruscans shared a banquet.

## The story

The Marquis Giampietro Campana found it at Cerveteri, the Etruscan city of Caere, in the Banditaccia necropolis in 1845. The Louvre bought the enormous Campana collection in 1861, which is the foundation of its Etruscan holdings.

A near-identical piece is in the Villa Giulia in Rome. The two are the same size and shape, almost certainly from the same workshop, and probably made within a few years of each other.

## Look for

- The gestures. Look at the hollow of each hand and work out what was held there.
- Her shoes, pointed and turned up at the toe, a distinctive Etruscan fashion. He is barefoot.
- The joins. The sarcophagus was made in four separately fired sections and assembled.
- The man's missing hand, lost on this version.
- Traces of paint. It was brightly coloured.

## Interesting facts

- Etruscan women of the elite dined reclining with men, owned property, and appear in inscriptions under their own names. Greek and Roman writers found this shocking and said so. This object is one of the main pieces of evidence for it.
- Firing a terracotta group this large without it cracking is a serious technical achievement. Hence the four parts.
- The Etruscan galleries are a few minutes from the Winged Victory and are almost always empty.

## Talking point

Nobody in Greek or Roman funerary art looks this happy. Ask why an Etruscan couple would want to be remembered mid-dinner.
