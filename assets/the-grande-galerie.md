# The Grande Galerie

Begun 1595. Denon wing, Level 1, Room 710.

## In one line

A corridor built to let a king walk from one palace to another without going outside, now hung with the best Italian paintings in France.

## What you are looking at

A long barrel-vaulted gallery running west along the Seine, lined with Italian painting from the fifteenth to the seventeenth century. This is where the Leonardos other than the Mona Lisa hang, along with Raphael, Mantegna, Ghirlandaio, Antonello and Correggio.

## The story

Catherine de' Medici built the Tuileries Palace to the west in 1564. Henri IV wanted the two palaces joined, and from 1595 built the Grande Galerie along the river to connect them. At 460 metres it was the longest room in Europe.

The ground floor below was given over to artists' and craftsmen's lodgings and workshops, by royal warrant. Painters, goldsmiths, clockmakers and instrument makers lived and worked under the gallery for two hundred years.

When the Louvre became a public museum in 1793, the Grande Galerie was the museum. Hubert Robert, the painter who was its first curator, painted both what it looked like and an imaginary view of it in ruins. Both pictures are in the Louvre.

The 1799 remodelling divided the endless corridor into bays with columns, which is what you walk through now, and later work added the overhead lighting.

### What happened here

- In 1606 the gallery was used as an indoor playroom for the five-year-old future Louis XIII on wet days. He was given a live fox to play with.
- Henri IV performed the royal touch here, laying hands on hundreds of people with scrofula, saying "The king touches you; God heals you."
- The Tuileries end burned in 1871 during the Commune. The palace was demolished in 1883, and the Grande Galerie now stops where it stopped.

## Look for

- The length. Stand at one end and look down it.
- The white marble columns that divide the bays, from the 1799 remodelling.
- The paintings hung on both sides, and the fact that the Leonardos are on the left as you walk west from the Salon Carré.

## Interesting facts

- At 460 metres it was the longest room in Europe when it was finished.
- Artists and craftsmen lived and worked in lodgings on the ground floor underneath, by royal warrant, for around two hundred years.
- Hubert Robert, the gallery's first curator, painted both a proposed redesign of it and an imaginary view of it as a ruin. Both paintings are in the Louvre.
- The 1799 remodelling broke the endless corridor into bays with columns. Before that it ran unbroken.
- The western end burned in 1871 during the Commune, and the Tuileries Palace it once connected to was demolished in 1883.

## Talking point

This corridor existed so a king would not have to cross a courtyard in the rain. It is now one of the great picture galleries of the world.
